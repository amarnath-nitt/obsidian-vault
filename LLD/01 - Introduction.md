# 1. Introduction to LLD

### 📝 Revision Notes
- **Definition**: Low-Level Design (LLD) focuses on the detailed component design of a system. It specifies the logic for each and every component of the system and how they interact. It's about designing classes, interfaces, methods, and their relationships.
- **Goal**: To create a detailed design that can be directly translated into code, ensuring maintainability, scalability, flexibility, and robustness.
- **Contrast with HLD**: High-Level Design (HLD) focuses on the overall system architecture, identifying major components and their interactions at a high level. LLD drills down into the internal structure of these components.

### 💬 Interview Q&A

> [!question] What is the primary goal of Low-Level Design?
> The primary goal of LLD is to define the internal logic of each system component, including class structures, interfaces, method signatures, and their relationships, in a way that facilitates direct coding while ensuring the system is maintainable, extensible, and robust.

> [!question] How does LLD differ from HLD?
> HLD (High-Level Design) focuses on the overall system architecture, identifying major components, their responsibilities, and how they interact at a macro level. LLD (Low-Level Design) takes these high-level components and designs their internal structure in detail, specifying classes, methods, and their relationships, often adhering to principles like SOLID and using design patterns.
